title: '"We have never been attacked": why we study small business security'
date: 2026-10-22
summary: 81% of small businesses reported a security incident in a year, yet the owners we spoke with kept saying they had never been attacked. What is behind that sentence?
tags: ['small business', 'usable security']
---

We started looking at small businesses because building a technical company has
become cheap. A handful of people can now combine a cloud database, hosted
identity, a few APIs, and some AI workflows into a product, and they handle user
data, regulated documents, code, and secrets from very early on. Security tends
to wait until the company can afford someone to own it. Meanwhile, most security
research and guidance is written for large organizations that already have a
security team.

What made us stop was a pair of numbers that did not fit. The Identity Theft
Resource Center's 2025 survey found that 81% of the small businesses it asked had
reported a security breach, a data breach, or both in the previous year [1]. Yet
in our interviews and surveys, with 128 participants in total, the sentence we
heard most from owners was "we have never been attacked."

Nobody was lying. The trouble is the word "attack." For many owners, an attack
means a dramatic breach, so suspicious logins and phishing emails do not count.
Many small businesses also have no logs, alerts, or monitoring, which means that
even when something does happen, it is hard to see and harder to remember. And
many assume that their cloud and SaaS providers have security covered. Put these
together, and "we have never been attacked" describes what the owner can see,
not what has happened.

This also explains something else we noticed. Security work in small businesses
rarely starts because of a threat. It starts because of funding, a large
customer's requirements, or a certification such as SOC 2 that becomes the price
of entering a market.

So we have come to think that small-business security is not enterprise security
made smaller. What these teams need is not a cheaper version of the enterprise
toolkit, but support that makes everyday risk visible before a breach story ever
exists [2].
